# Native scene assembly

## Build the cut

1. Confirm the selected take and duration for each shot from the shot log. Keep earlier attempts available. Use short shot names such as S01 through S06 after confirming the correct editor is open.
2. Open the native **Add media** menu and choose **New scene** when available. Name the new scene for the story. Verify the heading and editor controls before renaming.
3. Use **Add clip**, choose the intended project video by its exact visible title, and inspect the preview and trim. Apply **Add media** when shown. The first clip in an empty scene can open the picker directly.
4. Select a clip on the timeline to expose its insertion control. In an occupied scene, **Add clip** can open a menu containing **Add clip** and **Extend**. Choose **Add clip** for an existing project video. Inspect the current UI after each action.
5. Insert after the intended selected clip. Check the new clip and total duration before adding another. If the picker closes unexpectedly, inspect the timeline and duration first. Resolve whether insertion succeeded before repeating it.
6. Inspect each source trim in the picker. A source's earlier trim can carry over. Preserve the approved in/out points and verify the inserted length. Keep completed narration and its pause inside the retained range. If listening is unavailable, mark audio timing unverified and flag that limitation after trimming.
7. Zoom out enough to see the cut and count the clips. Inspect the sequence visually and check the total against the planned durations. For an accidental duplicate, use that timeline clip's context menu to remove the extra insertion, then verify the new total. Keep the source asset available.

Use current visible controls throughout. Timeline handles and insertion buttons can appear only after selecting or hovering over a clip. Re-observe the timeline when zoom or layout changes.

## Verify and deliver

Play the assembled scene from the start through the ending, with loop disabled when available. Check cuts, order, the hidden reveal, character and prop continuity, and the final state. Inspect exact speech and transitions by listening when the browser supports it. State what remains unverified.

Save with the native completion control, then reopen the scene through its visible card or its previously observed scene URL. Confirm the scene name, saved clip count, trims and total duration. A scene card can expose a clip count and filmstrip. Use them as persistence checks alongside the reopened timeline.

Leave the useful scene open for the creator and provide its actual URL. Report the final runtime, selected takes, any narration changes, verified charges and unresolved review. Capture a result screenshot according to the browser's guidance. Download or publish when requested.

For a missing shot blocked by the service or the budget, report the gap, its attempted recovery and the concrete decision needed. Keep completed clips usable and clearly distinguish a partial cut from a finished story.
